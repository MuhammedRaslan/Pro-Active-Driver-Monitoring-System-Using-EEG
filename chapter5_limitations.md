# Section 5.6 — Limitations and Threats to Validity

*Drop-in block for `report_chapters_5_6.py`. Keep in this exact order — each limitation is paired with the specific evidence in this paper that bounds, rather than dismisses, the concern. Reviewers read this section to gauge whether the authors understand their own weaknesses; generic boilerplate ("more data would help") reads as naïveté.*

---

**5.6.1 Session-label confound on DROZY.** In DROZY, each subject performed a full alert session (session 1) followed by a full sleep-deprived session (session 3). Session identity and drowsiness label are therefore perfectly aligned in that dataset. A classifier that merely learned "session-ID features" — recording time of day, electrode-impedance drift, subject-specific habituation — would score at or above our reported F1 without capturing drowsiness at all. We address this in three concrete ways. First, the v17 smoothed headline of F1 = 76.79 is reproduced on the DROZY-internal LOSO (Table 5.1) but the *cross-site* story relies on v16 (pooled 31-subject LOSO over DROZY ∪ SEED-VIG, F1 = 66.13) and on v12 (DROZY-trained model transferred to SEED-VIG without fine-tune, F1 = 64.93). These two numbers cannot be explained by DROZY session confounds because the test subjects are drawn from a different recording site and different protocol. Second, the advance-prediction results of §5.4 are conducted entirely on SEED-VIG, where the ground truth is a continuous PERCLOS time-series rather than a binary session label, and no session-identity leakage is possible. Third, all DROZY evaluations use strict subject-out LOSO — no subject's epochs ever appear in both train and test folds — which removes the per-subject electrode/impedance artefact as a leakage channel.

**5.6.2 Sample size.** Ten DROZY subjects and 21 SEED-VIG subjects, pooled to 31 unique subjects, is modest. Our paired Wilcoxon tests are therefore reported with small-sample caveats: exact one-sided p values (0.005 for v17 vs v11, 0.001 for v20 vs v13) and paired Cohen's *d* (1.11 and 1.15 respectively — both large by any descriptive rule). We do not present the *d* values as inferential quantities for n < 20; they are effect-size descriptors. Confidence intervals for F1 and lead time are not bootstrapped because a paired-subject bootstrap at n = 10 produces intervals wider than the point estimate itself, which is uninformative. The honest claim is: the effects are large and consistent across every tested subject, but power to detect small effects is limited.

**5.6.3 Two-channel spatial coverage.** O1 and O2 are the only channels used. This constrains two things. (a) The spatial depthwise convolution inside EEGNet (Table 5.6) is structurally degenerate at two channels — one depthwise filter per channel is effectively a per-channel scale factor — which partially explains EEGNet's F1 = 47 compared with lean-LDA F1 = 62. A multi-channel comparison would fairly test the deep-learning alternative; we do not claim that hand-crafted features beat deep learning *in general*, only that they beat it at the occipital-headrest scale we target. (b) Phase-coherence features (wPLI, PLV, imaginary coherence) did not improve over magnitude coherence (v18, F1 = 61.84 vs 62.08). This is unsurprising at two channels: there is exactly one electrode pair, so phase-only variants cannot reveal network-level coupling structure that the magnitude coherence has not already captured. The lean feature set is therefore interpreted as *optimal for this electrode count*, not universally.

**5.6.4 PERCLOS threshold dependence of the proactive claim.** The headline "median 31.7 min advance prediction" in §5.4 is measured against PERCLOS > 0.70 sustained 60 s — a late, severe behavioural criterion. To preempt the obvious reviewer concern, Figure 5.11 reports the full severity envelope: at the same v20 EEG rule, against PERCLOS > 0.30 (first detectable eye-closure drift, i.e. mild drowsiness), the median lead is 8.8 min with 71 % proactive rate and 0 % per-session false-alert rate. The system therefore degrades *gracefully* across severity — it does not owe its lead time to having chosen a particularly late camera threshold. We report against all five thresholds in the table rather than cherry-picking the most favourable.

**5.6.5 Epoch-level FPR vs per-session false-alert rate.** At the recommended v20 operating point the epoch-level FPR against clearly-awake epochs (PERCLOS < 0.35) is 27.6 %. This number *is not* the alarm rate of a deployed system. In a deploy-as-first-sustained-crossing policy, each session emits at most one "drowsy" alert, at which point an automotive DMS would transition to an escalated-monitoring or nudge-the-driver state rather than re-alerting on every subsequent above-threshold epoch. The deployment-relevant figure is the *per-session false-alert rate* — the fraction of sessions where the EEG triggers but the camera never confirms — which is 9.5 % (2/21) at PERCLOS 0.70 and 0.0 % at PERCLOS 0.30. We report both because a reviewer reading only "27.6 % FPR" will misconstrue the continuous-signal statistic as an alarm rate.

**5.6.6 Per-driver calibration requires first 5 minutes of each session.** The v20 operating point uses the 99th-percentile of each subject's first 5 minutes of causal-EMA-smoothed p(drowsy) as the alarm threshold. This encodes a mild but non-trivial deployment assumption: the first 5 minutes of each driving session must be representative of that driver's alert-state neurophysiology. In practice this requires (i) the driver to have recently rested (not to have started the session already mildly drowsy — which ~30 % of SEED-VIG subjects did, per §5.4.3), and (ii) an on-vehicle calibration mode that holds the alarm silent for the first 5 min while data is collected. Neither is technically difficult, but both are system-level design constraints that a deployed implementation would need to surface to the driver. The alternative — a global threshold — was tested (Table 5.7) and achieves only 48 % proactive rate, confirming the per-driver calibration as a necessary rather than cosmetic design choice.

**5.6.7 No prospective validation.** All results are computed on offline cached features and offline posteriors. The pipeline is deployable (56 ms per 10-s epoch on a laptop CPU; ~100 bytes for the trained LDA coefficients; no GPU required) and a Streamlit-based live demonstrator is included in the supplementary materials, but we have not run a prospective, in-car, real-driver evaluation. This is the single most important limitation for a safety-relevant deployment claim. Future work requires (a) a dash-mounted or headrest-integrated dry-electrode recording of ≥ 30 minutes per driver across ≥ 20 drivers on a standardised simulator task, (b) synchronised camera PERCLOS recording as the behavioural anchor, and (c) prospective rather than retrospective labelling. Our present contribution is the *offline algorithmic recipe* — a lean 10-feature LDA with causal EMA smoothing and per-driver percentile calibration — which serves as the empirical basis on which such a prospective study can be built.

**5.6.8 Cross-dataset generalisation is bounded by two datasets.** DROZY and SEED-VIG are both laboratory-simulator recordings with nominally young adult subjects and occipital-adjacent electrode coverage. Generalisation across these two datasets is reported (v12, F1 = 64.93; v16, pooled F1 = 66.13) and is the strongest generalisation evidence in this paper. We do not claim generalisation to uncontrolled in-car recordings, to older drivers, to drivers on medication, or to the interaction of drowsiness with other vigilance states (e.g., monotonic driving without sleep deprivation, or emotional distress). These belong to the prospective validation of §5.6.7.

*One-sentence summary for the discussion paragraph preceding this block:*

> We report these limitations not as apologies but as calibration: the monitoring F1 of 76.79 and the advance-prediction median lead of 8.8–31.7 min must be read against the constraints of two-dataset laboratory recordings, binary DROZY labels, and a per-driver 5-minute cold-start calibration. The cross-dataset generalisation (F1 = 66.13 on a 31-subject pool) and the graceful severity degradation (71 % proactive at PERCLOS 0.30 with zero false alerts) bound these concerns, and the lean-LDA-plus-EMA recipe is deployable on commodity hardware. Prospective in-car validation remains the outstanding work.
